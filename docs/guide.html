WattBench user guide

For WattBench 1.1 on iPhone (iOS 17 or later). Labels below are quoted exactly as they appear in the app.

Before you start

  • What you need: an iPhone running iOS 17 or later and a FNIRSI FNB58 USB power meter. Only the FNB58 is supported; its Bluetooth protocol carries voltage, current and power. D+/D− line voltages and temperature are shown on the meter itself and are not available over Bluetooth.
  • On the meter: open its settings menu and turn Bluetooth on. Keep it within a few metres of the phone.
  • No meter yet? Tap Try Demo Data on the Live tab (or in Settings → Advanced) to explore every screen with simulated readings. Demo readings are marked "Demo" everywhere and are excluded from the trip counter by default.

Connecting

  1. Tap Connect on the Live tab, or tap the status pill in the top-left corner. The Connect sheet opens and starts scanning. iOS asks for Bluetooth permission the first time.
  2. Meters named FNB58 appear under Nearby with a signal-strength glyph and their RSSI in dBm, strongest first. Tap one to connect.
  3. If exactly one FNB58 is found, WattBench connects to it by itself after a moment ("Found only this meter") and closes the sheet. Tap Cancel if you wanted a different one.

WattBench remembers the last meter. With Auto-connect on launch on (the default), it connects to that meter every time you open the app, and the title menu offers Reconnect to Last Meter.

The status pill

  • Not connected, Scanning, Connecting — before a link is up. Tap the pill at any time to open the Connect sheet.
  • FNB58 (green) — connected and receiving readings. No data replaces it when nothing has arrived for two seconds.
  • Reconnecting (orange, with a timer) — the meter dropped out. WattBench reconnects as soon as it is back in range or powered on, even with the phone locked. Readings, trip and chart stay on screen.
  • Unreachable (red) — two minutes have passed without the meter. A Retry button appears. If a recording is running, the reconnect is kept pending so a returning meter resumes the same recording with a gap.
  • Bluetooth off / No access — fix it from the Connect sheet's Open Settings button.
  • Demo (purple) — simulated readings.

Connect sheet extras

  • Scan / Stop in the toolbar; Scan Again after nothing is found for eight seconds.
  • Advanced → Show all Bluetooth devices lists every advertising device (with a "Device name" search field) instead of only names containing FNB58.
  • Forget meter stops auto-connect on launch. You can pick the meter again from the list later.
  • The title menu on the Live tab (tap the "WattBench" title) also has Connect…, Disconnect and Try Demo Data.

The Live tab

Readouts

  • The large readout shows one metric (Power by default). The two tiles below show the others. Tap a tile to make it the large readout.
  • Voltage shows its range since you connected (for example "9.01–9.05 V"); current and power show their peak and the time it happened ("PEAK 27.4 W · 14:02:11"). Peaks persist until you reset them.
  • Long-press any readout for Reset Peaks and Copy Value.
  • Units auto-range: current and power switch to mA/mW below about 1 A/1 W and back above it; energy shows mWh below 1 Wh. Turn this off in Settings → Auto-range units.

Trip counter

  • The TRIP card accumulates energy (Wh) and capacity (mAh) plus active time, peak and average power since it was last reset, across reconnects and app launches. No recording needed.
  • Reset clears it (you are asked to confirm above 0.01 Wh). Long-press for Reset Trip and Copy Values.
  • A pause glyph shows when no data has arrived for five seconds. Gaps longer than five seconds are not counted. Demo readings are not counted while Exclude demo readings from trip is on.

Chart

  • Pick V, A, W or All and a window of 10 s, 30 s, 60 s or 2 min. The window you pick here is remembered.
  • Touch and hold the plot to read the exact value and time under your finger. Drag to scroll back through the last two minutes; the Live button returns to now.
  • Gray bands mark dropouts (samples more than five seconds apart). A dashed line marks the peak; markers from the current recording appear as labelled lines.
  • The … menu has Show Peak Line, Copy Value Under Cursor and Clear Chart (clears only the live history).

Recording

Start

  1. Tap Record in the bar at the bottom (enabled once a meter or demo is connected).
  2. The Recording sheet suggests a name ("FNB58 · Sep 26 at 8:24 AM"); change it if you like, add tag chips (Charger, Cable, Power bank, Device or your recent tags) and a Note.
  3. Optionally set a rule under Stop automatically, then tap Start Recording. The rule you used becomes the default for next time.

Auto-stop rules

  • When current stays below a threshold (default 100 mA) For 30 s, 1 min, 2 min or 5 min. Only time with live readings counts, so a reconnect gap never triggers it. The timer resets once current rises 2% above the threshold.
  • After duration: 30 min, 1 h, 2 h, 4 h or 8 h of active recording time (gaps excluded).
  • At energy: a target in Wh.
  • Rules work with the screen locked. When one fires, the session is saved and a marker such as "Auto-stop: current below threshold" is added. Allow notifications (see Alerts) to be told when it stops.

While recording

  • The bar shows a pulsing red dot, the elapsed time, and the energy and capacity captured so far. The Sessions tab shows a small red chip that jumps back to Live.
  • Markers: tap the bookmark button to add "Mark 1", "Mark 2", …; hold it for Plugged in, Unplugged, Cable swapped, Load changed or Custom…. Markers appear on the live chart and in the session report, and energy is integrated between consecutive markers.
  • Lock the phone or switch apps freely: with a real meter the recording continues in the background. Demo recordings run only while the app is open.
  • Every sample is written to disk as it arrives (flushed at least once a second and forced to disk every ten seconds), so a crash loses about a second at most.
  • The … menu shows the armed auto-stop rule and offers Discard Recording….

Stop

  • Tap the round Stop button. The session is saved immediately and a toast shows "Saved · <energy>" with Undo for five seconds (Undo deletes the session).
  • If no samples arrived at all, WattBench asks whether to discard or keep recording.
  • Bluetooth dropouts are recorded as "Gap 12 s" markers and shown as gaps in the chart; they are not counted in energy, capacity or active time.

If WattBench closes mid-recording

On the next launch a Recording was interrupted sheet shows the energy, capacity, duration and sample count written so far. Choose Keep Recording to save it as a recovered session (marked with an orange triangle) or Discard. The Sessions list also offers Keep / Discard for it.

Sessions

The list

  • Sessions are grouped by day. Each row shows the name, start time, active duration, energy, tags and a small power sparkline; demo sessions carry a purple DEMO badge.
  • Search by name, device, tag or note. Sort by Newest, Longest or Most energy.
  • Swipe right to Rename; swipe left to Share or Delete. Long-press for a preview with Share CSV, Copy Summary, Rename and Delete. Edit selects several sessions to share or delete together.

The report

  • Tap the title to rename it. Use the tags row to add or edit tags, and Add a note under Notes.
  • Tiles: ENERGY, CAPACITY and DURATION (active time and wall-clock time). Highlights lists time-weighted average power, peak power, the voltage and current ranges and, if any, gaps not integrated.
  • Chart: V, A or W at full resolution with a shaded min/max envelope when zoomed out. Pinch to zoom (down to 10 seconds), drag to scroll, touch and hold to read a value. Presets: All, 1 h, 10 min, 1 min.
  • Range: tap Range, then drag across the chart. A card shows the selection's duration, energy (and its share of the session), capacity, average V/A/W, voltage and current ranges and peak power. Save as Marker Pair keeps the selection as "Range start" / "Range end" markers.
  • Markers: every marker with its time, the energy to the next marker and the readings at that moment. Tap one to jump the chart there; swipe to delete markers you added.
  • Integrity: sample count and rate, gaps, recording times, meter name, auto-stop reason, and whether the session was recovered.

Export

  • Tap Share in a session (or swipe a row) to export a CSV through the iOS share sheet: AirDrop it to a Mac, save it to Files, or send it to any app. Copy Summary puts a one-line summary ("name · energy · duration · peak") on the clipboard.
  • CSV columns: timestamp (UTC, ISO 8601 with milliseconds), voltage_v, current_a, power_w, elapsed_s, marker_label. It opens directly in Numbers, Excel, or pandas.
  • Recordings live in WattBench's own Documents folder, which you can browse in the Files app (On My iPhone → WattBench). The files there are binary; CSV is the readable format.

Alerts

  • Open Settings → Alert rules. Add a preset (USB-C 65 W, 5 V device, Power bank drain) or your own rule with a condition: Over-voltage, Under-voltage, Over-current, Over-power, Current below (for a duration), Voltage drop (within one second) or Disconnected (for a duration, only while recording).
  • Rules are checked on every reading, recording or not. Each fires at most once a minute and re-arms once the value is back 2% inside its bound.
  • While WattBench is open, an alert shows as a red banner with a haptic. In the background it arrives as a notification (turn on Notify in the background for the rule); during a recording the notification offers Stop & save. A fired alert also adds a marker to the running recording.
  • WattBench asks for notification permission the first time you enable a rule, never before. Send test notification checks that it works.

Settings

Open Settings with the gear on the Live tab.

  • Display: Hero metric (Voltage / Current / Power); Auto-range units; Precision (3 or 4 digits); Capacity (mAh / Auto); Haptic feedback; Keep screen awake while connected (ignored in Low Power Mode).
  • Charts: Default window (10 sec / 30 sec / 1 min / 2 min); Show peak line.
  • Recording: the remembered Default auto-stop rule (with Clear default auto-stop); Exclude demo readings from trip.
  • Alerts: Alert rules.
  • Meter: Last meter; Auto-connect on launch; Show all Bluetooth devices; Forget meter. Bluetooth stays active in the background only while WattBench is connected to a meter, so a recording continues with the screen locked. Disconnect when you are done to save battery. WattBench never scans for meters in the background.
  • Storage: session count and space used; Delete All Sessions (export anything you want to keep first).
  • Advanced: Diagnostics; Try demo data / Stop demo data.
  • About: version, privacy policy, support and source code links.

Quick actions and diagnostics

  • Home Screen quick actions: press and hold the WattBench icon for Start Recording (opens the recording sheet once a meter is connected) and Connect to Last Meter.
  • Diagnostics (Settings → Advanced) shows the connection state, frame counters, the recording journal, state restoration, the meter's Bluetooth services and a log. Copy or Share it when reporting a problem.

Troubleshooting

"No Meter Found"

Make sure Bluetooth is enabled in the meter's own settings menu and the meter is within a few metres. Only devices named FNB58 are listed unless Show all Bluetooth devices is on. If the meter is connected to another phone or a computer, disconnect it there first.

The app sits on "Connecting to FNB58"

Auto-connect waits for the remembered meter, which is probably switched off. Turn it on, or open Settings → Forget meter and connect from the list instead.

"No data" while connected

The meter is connected but not sending. Check that the meter is on its measurement screen and something is plugged into it; open Diagnostics to see whether frames are arriving.

Readings stop when I lock the phone

They shouldn't with a real meter: WattBench keeps the connection alive in the background. Demo readings do pause in the background. If iOS relaunched the app in the background, it re-adopts the meter but does not resume a recording.

An alert never arrives in the background

Check Settings → Alert rules → Notifications shows "Allowed", and that the rule's Notify in the background is on. Notifications are only sent while WattBench is not in front.

Something else

Copy the Diagnostics text and open an issue on GitHub.
