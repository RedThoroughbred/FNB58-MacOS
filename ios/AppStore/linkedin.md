## About

I'm a Python developer at Car-Part.com, where my work has shifted from internal tooling to AI:
consulting inside the company on where AI genuinely helps (and where it doesn't), training teams
to use it well, and building with Claude Code every day. I wrote "The AI Choice: Into the Light,
Into the Dark" about exactly that fork in the road.

The best way I know to stay honest about what AI tools can do is to ship real software with them.
On nights and weekends I build iPhone apps for things I actually do, and two are on the App Store
as of this year:

**Repot: Plant Care** (Aug 2026) — the whole workbench for a home nursery, not another watering
reminder. Every plant tracked as an individual, a daily "round" that walks care thirstiest-first,
photo timelines with side-by-side comparison, season-aware schedules that explain themselves, a
400+ plant encyclopedia that works offline, and first-class support for worm bins. Free, with
optional paid features.

**WattBench** (Sep 2026) — a Bluetooth companion for the FNIRSI FNB58 USB power meter, built for
the electronics bench: live voltage/current/power on an instrument-style face, a trip counter for
energy and capacity, recordings that keep running with the screen locked and survive a crash,
session reports with zoomable charts and range statistics, alerts, CSV export. Native SwiftUI and
CoreBluetooth; nothing leaves the phone. The desktop companion and protocol notes are open source.

What ties it together: a real problem, a bias toward finishing — App Store review, privacy,
pricing, the unglamorous last 10% — and an AI-first workflow. Both apps were built with Claude
Code as a daily collaborator: I own the product decisions, the architecture and the testing, and
I use the tools the way I teach other people to use them.

Stack: Python at work; Swift, SwiftUI, Swift Charts and CoreBluetooth on the side; Claude Code
throughout.
