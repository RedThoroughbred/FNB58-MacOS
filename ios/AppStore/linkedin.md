## About (2,600 characters max)

I like building tools for things I actually do — and then shipping them properly.

In the last three months I designed, built and released two iPhone apps under my own account:

**Repot: Plant Care** (App Store, August 2026). Most plant apps are a watering reminder with a
logo. Repot is a workbench for people who run a real growing collection: every plant tracked as
an individual, a daily "round" that walks you through care thirstiest-first, a photo timeline per
plant with side-by-side comparisons, season-aware watering and feeding schedules that explain
themselves, a 400+ plant encyclopedia that works fully offline, and — because nobody else does it —
first-class support for worm bins and vermicomposting. Free, with optional paid features.

**WattBench** (App Store, September 2026). A Bluetooth companion for the FNIRSI FNB58 USB power
meter, built for the electronics bench. Live voltage, current and power on an instrument-style face,
a trip counter for energy and capacity, recordings that keep running with the screen locked and
survive a crash, session reports with zoomable charts and range statistics, threshold alerts, CSV
export. Native SwiftUI + CoreBluetooth; nothing leaves the phone. The desktop companion and the
reverse-engineered protocol notes are open source on GitHub.

What both projects have in common: a real user (me) with a real problem, a bias toward finishing —
App Store review, privacy manifests, screenshots, pricing, the unglamorous last 10% — and a stack I
know well: Swift, SwiftUI, Swift Charts, CoreBluetooth, local-first data, no accounts, no tracking.

Day job: [one line about what you do now]. Off the clock: houseplants, worm bins, ham radio and a
bench full of USB meters.
